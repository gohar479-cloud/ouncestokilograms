What Are an Ounce, a Troy Ounce, and a Kilogram?

An ounce is a small unit of mass that comes in two common forms. The standard ounce weighs 28.35 grams, and the troy ounce for precious metals weighs 31.10 grams. A kilogram is the metric base unit of mass equal to 1,000 grams.

The Standard (Avoirdupois) Ounce

The avoirdupois ounce is a unit of mass that equals one sixteenth of a pound and measures everyday goods. The word avoirdupois (said "av-er-duh-POIZ") comes from old French words meaning "goods of weight." Shoppers in the United States see this ounce on almost every food package. One avoirdupois ounce holds 16 drams, and a dram is a tiny unit used in old recipes.

The United Kingdom adopted the same 1959 pound through the Weights and Measures Act 1963. As a result, a British ounce and an American ounce weigh the same today. Steak menus in many UK restaurants proudly list cuts in ounces, and shops sell the same meat in grams.

The Troy Ounce

The troy ounce (oz t) is a unit of mass that equals 31.1034768 grams and is used to weigh gold, silver, platinum, and palladium. The name comes from Troyes, a market town in France where medieval traders weighed silver and spices. One troy ounce holds 480 grains, while a standard ounce holds 437.5 grains.

The troy system builds its units from the same grain. According to NIST Handbook 44, 24 grains make 1 pennyweight, and 20 pennyweights make 1 troy ounce. Twelve troy ounces form 1 troy pound of 373.24 grams. So a troy pound is lighter than a standard pound, even though a troy ounce is heavier than a standard ounce.

Troy ounces convert to kilograms with their own factor. Multiply troy ounces by 0.0311034768 to get kilograms. A 10 oz t silver bar weighs 0.3110 kg, and one kilogram holds 32.1507 troy ounces. The converter's Troy setting uses this exact factor.

The Kilogram

The kilogram is the base unit of mass in the International System of Units (SI) and anchors every metric weight. The General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) voted in November 2018 to change how the kilogram is defined. The new rule began on 20 May 2019, which is World Metrology Day.

Before that date, one metal cylinder near Paris set the size of the kilogram. Scientists called that platinum and iridium cylinder "Le Grand K." Today, the kilogram rests on the Planck constant, a fixed number from physics equal to 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ joule-seconds. The International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM) chose that number so the kilogram kept its old size. That choice keeps every ounce's value the same.

Scientists separate mass from weight. Mass is the amount of matter in an object, and weight is the pull of gravity on it. Shoppers use both words for the same idea, and this guide does the same.

How Do You Convert Ounces to Kilograms?

Multiply the number of ounces by 0.028349523125 to get kilograms. Dividing the ounces by 35.27396195 gives the same result.

Why Is the Factor 0.028349523125?

The factor comes from splitting one pound into 16 equal parts. The 1959 agreement fixed the pound at 0.45359237 kg, so one ounce is that value divided by 16.

0.45359237 ÷ 16 = 0.028349523125

NIST confirms the same figure. Its Handbook 44, Appendix C lists one avoirdupois ounce as exactly 28.349523125 grams. Because both numbers are exact by law, the factor never changes from year to year.

Two Worked Examples

Picture a home cook with a US brownie recipe that calls for 10 ounces of chocolate. Ten ounces times 0.0283495 equals 0.2835 kg, or about 283 grams on a kitchen scale. An online seller shipping a candle that weighs 28 ounces gets 0.7938 kg for the customs form.

What Is the Reverse Factor?

One kilogram equals 35.27396195 ounces. To turn kilograms back into ounces, multiply the kilograms by that number. A traveler whose airline allows 23 kg can turn that limit into ounces: 23 × 35.274 = 811.3 ounces. The swap button in the converter does this step for you.

Many European cookbooks print 28 grams per ounce as a shortcut. The exact factor is about 1.2% more accurate, which helps bread and pastry turn out right.

What Is the Step-by-Step Method?

To convert oz to kg by hand, follow 4 steps: write the ounces, multiply, round, and check. The whole method takes less than a minute with a phone calculator.

  1. Write the weight in ounces as a decimal number, such as 15 or 7.5.
  2. Multiply that number by 0.028349523125, or by 0.0283495 for most uses.
  3. Round the answer to fit your task, such as 2 decimal places for cooking.
  4. Check the answer against a known value, such as 16 oz = 0.4536 kg.

Here is how to convert oz to kg with a real number. Fifteen ounces times 0.0283495 equals 0.4252 kg. That answer sits a little below 16 ounces, so it passes the check. For results in grams, the ounces-to-grams converter answers directly.

Worked Decimal Examples

School problems often use decimals, so here are 3 practice examples. To convert 90.2 oz to kg, multiply 90.2 by 0.028349523125 and get 2.5571 kg. The same steps convert 91.3 oz to kg and give 2.5883 kg. A third worksheet may ask you to convert 92.2 oz to kg, and the answer is 2.6138 kg.

Teachers call this method dimensional analysis. Dimensional analysis is a way of solving unit problems by multiplying by a fraction equal to 1, such as 0.0283495 kg per 1 oz. The ounce units cancel, and only kilograms remain.

Mental Math Shortcut

Divide the ounces by 35 for a fast estimate. For 105 ounces, 105 ÷ 35 = 3 kg, and the exact answer is 2.9767 kg. The estimate lands within about 0.8% of the exact value. That works well in a grocery aisle, and the exact factor suits postage labels best.

Pounds Plus Ounces

Turn the pounds into ounces first, add the extra ounces, and then multiply. A parcel of 2 lb 5 oz holds 2 × 16 + 5 = 37 ounces. Thirty-seven ounces times 0.0283495 equals 1.0489 kg. For weights given only in pounds, the pounds-to-kilograms calculator is faster.

Which Everyday Things Do People Convert From Ounces to Kilos?

People convert 5 main groups of everyday items: food, mail, newborn babies, luggage, and fitness gear. Gold and silver form a sixth group, but they use a different ounce.

Food Labels and Recipes

US food labels show both units by law. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) sets this rule in 21 CFR 101.105. Packaged food must list its net weight in metric units and in US customary units. Net weight is the weight of the food alone, without the box, can, or jar.

NIST studied how well stores follow this rule. Researchers examined at least 1,137 packages in 19 retail stores. About 17% of those packages listed metric units only, which shows how familiar metric labels have become. Random-weight packages, such as deli meat priced by the pound, are exempt from the two-unit rule.

A home cook working from an American recipe meets these numbers in every aisle. One stick of butter weighs 4 ounces, which is 0.1134 kg. A yogurt cup often holds 6 ounces, and a 6 oz to kg check gives 0.1701 kg. Canned beans usually come in 15-ounce cans, so 15 oz to kg equals 0.4252 kg.

Larger packages need the same care. Big cans of crushed tomatoes hold 28 ounces, which comes to 0.7938 kg. A tall canister of rolled oats often weighs 18 ounces, or 0.5103 kg. Bakers who look up 16 oz to kg for a pound of flour get 0.4536 kg.

Sandwich loaves often weigh 24 ounces, which gives 0.6804 kg. A large jar of mayonnaise may hold 30 ounces by weight, or 0.8505 kg. Shoppers in metric countries compare price per kilogram, so this figure helps them judge value.

Mail and Shipping

The United States Postal Service (USPS) uses 2 key ounce limits. Its Domestic Mail Manual caps a First-Class letter at 3.5 ounces, which is 0.0992 kg. First-Class Mail of any shape stops at 13 ounces. The 13 oz to kg value is 0.3685 kg, and heavier pieces move to other services.

Small sellers on Etsy and eBay work with these limits every day and price their parcels with confidence. A greeting card with a thin gift may weigh 2 ounces, or 0.0567 kg. A 3 oz to kg check gives 0.0850 kg, which still fits the letter limit. A paperback book of 11 ounces weighs 0.3118 kg, so it stays under the 13-ounce cap.

Many international carriers bill by the next half kilogram or full kilogram. Sellers get the smoothest delivery by rounding up before printing a label. Here is where it gets nuanced: a carrier may bill by box size instead of weight for light, bulky parcels.

Newborn and Child Weight

US hospitals often report birth weight in pounds and ounces, and most of the world uses kilograms. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) defines low birthweight as less than 2,500 grams. In US units, that border is written as 5 pounds 8 ounces.

Nurses and parents use the pounds-plus-ounces method for any baby weight. A newborn of 7 lb 8 oz weighs 7 × 16 + 8 = 120 ounces. One hundred twenty ounces times 0.0283495 equals 3.4019 kg. Grandparents abroad often want this kilogram figure in the first message from the hospital.

The border value is an interesting special case. Exactly 5 lb 8 oz equals 88 ounces, or 2.4948 kg. That number sits 5 grams below 2,500 grams. Health agencies use the rounded pair, so doctors read both forms as the same line.

From the Grocery Ounce to the Bullion Ounce

Every value in the chart so far uses one ounce: the grocery ounce of 28.35 grams. That ounce fills shopping carts, mailbags, and baby books across the United States. But the word "ounce" has a second life in jewelry stores and bank vaults. There, the ounce weighs a little more and carries its own name. And this is the step that makes this guide complete. Choosing the right ounce matters, because the two differ by almost 10%. For gold buyers, the right ounce protects the value of every purchase. The next lines explain the bullion ounce in plain numbers.

Is a Troy Ounce the Same as a Regular Ounce?

No. A troy ounce weighs 31.1034768 grams, while a regular ounce weighs 28.349523125 grams. The troy ounce is about 9.7% heavier.

A regular ounce holds 437.5 grains, so the troy ounce carries 42.5 more grains. The converter's Ounce setting uses the regular ounce. Gold and silver weights need the Troy setting instead.

kg = oz t × 0.0311034768

To convert gold oz to kg, multiply troy ounces by 0.0311034768. One kilogram holds 32.1507 troy ounces, so 1 kg to troy oz equals 32.15 oz t. The troy ounce to kilogram converter handles bullion math with this factor.

Why the Troy setting matters. A gold investor buying a 5 oz t bar gets 5 × 0.0311034768 = 0.1555 kg. The standard setting would show 0.1417 kg for the same 5 ounces. That 13.8-gram gap is real money for bullion buyers.

The London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) sets the world standard for large bars. Its Good Delivery rules require a gold bar to hold 350 to 430 fine troy ounces. That range equals about 10.9 kg to 13.4 kg. A standard silver bar holds about 1,000 troy ounces, which is close to 31.1 kg.

The Perth Mint, owned by the Government of Western Australia, notes one common habit. Dealers commonly print "oz" on coins and bars as a short form of troy ounces. Buyers can confidently read every precious metal "oz" as a troy ounce.

Does a Fluid Ounce Weigh the Same as an Ounce?

No. A fluid ounce measures volume, or the space a liquid fills, while an ounce measures mass. One US fluid ounce holds 29.57 milliliters.

Water is the one easy case. One US fluid ounce of water weighs about 29.6 grams, which is close to 1.04 ounces. So a 32-ounce water bottle filled with water weighs about 0.946 kg, a bit more than 32 ounces of weight (0.907 kg). That 39-gram difference is a handy fact for hikers and gym members.

Other liquids break the water pattern. Cooking oil is lighter than water, and honey is much heavier. A jar labeled in fluid ounces needs the liquid's density, which is its mass per milliliter, before any kilogram answer is exact. Most bottles in US stores print "fl oz," so the letters "fl" are the signal to watch.
